\section{Label Noise: Whose Guarantee Is It?}
\label{sec:label-noise}

A conformal guarantee is a statement about the labels it was calibrated
against. If those labels are wrong, the guarantee still holds, but it is a
guarantee about agreement with the labeller rather than about missed
defects. The primary dataset's labels were bootstrapped with GPT-4V and
published on Roboflow, the usual route by which inspection datasets come
into being. We measured how far that route can be trusted.

\paragraph{Agreement with a hand-labelled gold set.}
We hand-labelled every CBRI image outside the training split ($n = 175$)
without seeing the bootstrap annotations. Agreement on the binary
defect/no-defect label is at chance: Cohen's $\kappa = -0.007$. Per class,
$\kappa$ ranges from $-0.02$ (corrosion) to $0.29$ (vegetation), with
crack at $0.16$. The disagreement is one-sided. Of the 74 images the
bootstrap leaves unannotated (and a pipeline would therefore treat as
defect-free), 73 contain a defect. Overall the bootstrap misses $42\%$ of
gold defects (73 of 172), and it almost never invents one (2 of 175).

\paragraph{Which defects are missed matters more than how many.}
Hidden defects affect the two guarantees we study differently.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Joint} risk, $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}\{\text{defect} \wedge
  \text{cleared}\}] \le \varepsilon$: a hidden defect looks free to clear,
  so the realised risk inflates by roughly $1/(1-h)$ for a hidden fraction
  $h$, whichever defects are hidden.
  \item \emph{Conditional} risk, $\Pr(\text{cleared} \mid \text{defect})
  \le \varepsilon$, calibrated on labelled defects only: if defects are
  hidden at random, the labelled ones remain an exchangeable sample of all
  defects and the guarantee survives, at a smaller $n$. If the hidden
  defects are the ones the model also finds hard, it fails.
\end{itemize}
On CBRI the bootstrap's misses do not look harder: the defects it missed
receive the same S1 and S2 scores as those it annotated (one-sided
Mann--Whitney $p = 0.71$ and $0.90$).

\paragraph{Degradation on real labels.}
Within the 175 gold images we calibrated CRC on the bootstrap labels and
measured the realised miss rate against gold (100 random 100/75 splits).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Joint guarantee: broken.} At $\varepsilon = 0.05$ the realised
  risk is $0.093$ (S1) and $0.101$ (S2), i.e. $1.85\times$ and $2.0\times$ the
  promised bound. It exceeds $\varepsilon$ in $86$--$90\%$ of deployments.
  Calibrated on gold, the same pipeline realises $0.052$.
  \item \textbf{Conditional guarantee: survives.} The realised risk is $0.042$
  (S1) and $0.052$ (S2) at $\varepsilon = 0.05$. This is consistent with the
  missing-at-random pattern above.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Controlled degradation.}
On SDNET2018, whose human labels we treat as ground truth, we hid a
fraction $h$ of defects in the calibration labels only, up to CBRI's
observed $h = 0.42$, either at random or worst-case (the most
clean-looking defects first). With random hiding the joint risk grows
smoothly to $1.7$--$2.0\times\varepsilon$ at $h = 0.42$, and the conditional
risk stays at or below $\varepsilon$ throughout. With worst-case hiding,
$h = 0.10$ already pushes the conditional miss rate to $0.14$ at
$\varepsilon = 0.05$ ($2.9\times$). At $h = 0.42$ it reaches $0.45$, i.e.\
nearly half of all real defects are auto-cleared under a nominal $5\%$
guarantee.

\paragraph{Consequence.}
Every headline number in this paper is calibrated on human labels: gold
for CBRI, the original annotations for SDNET2018. A pipeline calibrated
on bootstrapped labels inherits their errors silently, with no symptom
visible from inside the pipeline. Under the joint guarantee, labels like
CBRI's double the realised miss rate. Under the conditional guarantee,
the damage depends entirely on whether the labeller's misses correlate
with the model's. That cannot be checked without a gold set.
